Order flow explains crypto price moves.
It doesn't predict them.

When more pressure hits the buy side of the order book than the sell side, the price rises in the same 10 seconds, reliably. But that pressure says almost nothing about where the price goes in the next 10 seconds. This page shows the evidence from tick-level data on BTC, ETH and WLD futures, tested against hypotheses written down before any result was seen.

How it works

Order-flow imbalance (OFI), from Cont, Kukanov & Stoikov (2014), measures net buying pressure at the best prices. Three steps:

  1. 1

    Watch the top of the book

    Every change to the best bid (highest buy order) and best ask (lowest sell order) on Binance is recorded: millions of updates per symbol per day.

  2. 2

    Score each change

    New buy orders, or sell orders being cancelled or filled, count as + pressure. The mirror events count as − pressure. Summing over 10 seconds gives the OFI.

  3. 3

    Compare with the price

    Explain: does OFI line up with the price change in the same 10 s? Predict: does it forecast the next 10 s, using only data you would really have had?

The exact definition

For each top-of-book update n, with best bid price/size Pb, qb and best ask Pa, qa:

en = 1{Pbn ≥ Pbn−1} qbn − 1{Pbn ≤ Pbn−1} qbn−1 − 1{Pan ≤ Pan−1} qan + 1{Pan ≥ Pan−1} qan−1

OFIk = Σ en over bucket k, and each 30-minute window is fitted as ΔMidk = α + β·OFIk + ε (Newey–West errors). Prices are in ticks, sizes in coins. Funding times (00/08/16 UTC ± 2 min) are excluded.

Does it predict?

The prediction test uses only what a trader could have known: data timed by when it arrived, plus 100 ms of latency before the forecast window starts. One slope per symbol was fitted on 24 training days, checked on 7 validation days, then frozen and run once on 6 held-out days (April–September 2026).

How this was kept honest

  1. Before any resultHypotheses frozen. Pass criteria, sample, train/validation/test split and inference written down and committed.
  2. Data sourceNative Binance quotes chosen over a batched alternative, on fidelity to the method and before any result.
  3. Explain, H1–H4Run on BTC, then ETH and a third symbol. The third (WLD) was picked by a fixed rule on a date before the sample began.
  4. Predict, H5–H6Timing rule logged before code. A property test proves the forecast target never overlaps its input.
  5. One shotHeld-out days opened once. The data loader refuses them everywhere else. Results were committed whatever they showed.

Limitations
One day per month, one venue, one simple model at one 10-second horizon. A null here doesn't rule out OFI as one input among many, or for passive (maker) execution.
